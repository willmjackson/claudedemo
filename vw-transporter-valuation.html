Sweet spot 150 PS is the most popular for owner-drivers. The jump from 250 Nm to 340 Nm torque is huge. Single turbo = simpler, more reliable than biturbo. Gets the 6-speed manual (5-speed on 90/110 is the weak link).

199 vs 204 The 199 PS (2020) was detuned from 204 to meet Euro 6d. Re-rated to 204 PS from ~2021. Identical in practice. Both DSG-only.

Biturbo The pre-facelift T6 204 PS (CXEB) had turbo/oil issues. The T6.1 biturbo (CXEC) is improved but inherently more complex. Budget for higher long-term maintenance.

Options

Automatic (DSG)

The DQ500 7-speed wet-clutch DSG. Robust when maintained — oil + filter change every 37k miles is critical. Neglecting this causes expensive mechatronic failure. Budget ~£300 for the service at an independent. Commands a significant used premium (~£6.7k in this dataset), especially for campervans.

Note 90 PS and 110 PS are manual-only. DSG is optional on 150 PS and standard on 199/204 PS.

High Roof

Adds ~490 mm internal height (1,940 mm vs 1,410 mm) and 2.6 m³ extra load volume. LWB only. Great for standing height in campervan conversions. Trade-off: higher centre of gravity, worse fuel economy, restricted in some car parks and ferries.

4Motion AWD

Haldex-type on-demand system — front-biased, engages rear axle when slip is detected. Adds ~100-120 kg kerb weight (reduces payload). Excellent for mud, snow, wet grass. Only available with 150 PS+ engines. Pushes you towards T30/T32 for payload headroom. Highly sought after for campervans.
